# Selling Jamavat in Surat

The first ten customers come from a scooter and a phone, not from Google.
This is the list to walk, what to say, and what to do after they say
"vichaarine kahu chhu" (I'll think about it).

---

## 1. Who to walk into first

Jamavat is worth real money to a restaurant that has **all four** of these.
Anything missing and you are selling against the owner's own experience:

| | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **8+ dine-in tables**, busy at lunch or dinner | Below that the owner takes every order himself and feels no pain |
| **Two or more kitchens** (Gujarati + Punjabi, or a live counter) | Item-wise splitting is the one thing the ₹300 QR-menu apps cannot do |
| **No POS yet**, or paper chits | Someone already paying Petpooja needs a reason to switch; someone on chits needs a reason to start |
| **The owner is on the floor** | He decides in one conversation. A manager has to ask someone, and that someone never calls back |

Thali and dining halls are the best fit of all: fixed menu, two kitchens,
heavy lunch rush, and a bill that is the same every time — the demo takes
four minutes and he sees his own restaurant in it.

Skip for now: tea stalls, single-counter fast food, pure takeaway, and
anything inside a mall food court (the mall's own billing is mandated).

## 3. Getting the numbers (20 minutes, once per area)

1. Google Maps → search `restaurants in Adajan Surat` → zoom to the area.
2. Left list: open each one and read **Phone**, **rating + review count**, and
   the photos (photos tell you the table count and whether there is a counter).
3. Anything with 4.0+ and 200+ reviews goes on the list; under 50 reviews
   usually means too small.
4. Put it straight into **HQ → Leads → Add a lead** — restaurant, phone, area,
   tables, and the day to come back. That is the list you work from, not a
   notebook.

Only the business's own published number — never a personal mobile from a
WhatsApp group or a directory's "owner" field.

## 4. What to say

**At the door, to whoever is at the counter (10 seconds):**
> "Malik sahib chhe? Hu Jamavat mathi — restaurant mate QR menu banaviye
> chhiye. Be minute joi lo, pachhi na game to kahi dejo."

**The 30-second pitch, phone in hand, menu already open:**
> Guest table par baithine QR scan kare, potana phone par menu jue, order
> aape — order sidho kitchen ma jaay. Tamara be kitchen hoy to Gujarati
> ni dish Gujarati kitchen ma ane Punjabi ni Punjabi ma, alag alag screen par.
> Jamyaa pachhi bill counter par taiyar — koi chitthi ganvani nahi.
> Waiter ne vaar vaar bolavvo na pade, etle table jaldi khali thay.

Then **hand them the phone** and let them scan the QR themselves. The demo
sells; the sentence only buys you the phone.

**The four objections, and the honest answer to each:**

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Mara customer scan nahi karshe" | "Menu card pan rahe che — QR extra option chhe. Je scan kare tenu order sidhu kitchen ma. Ek table par try karo." |
| "Petpooja chhe already" | "Petpooja billing sarsu chhe. Aa QR ordering chhe — guest potej order aape, ane item-wise kitchen ma vahechaay. Jo billing badlvi na hoy to QR thi j shuru karo." |
| "Kharcho ketlo?" | Price only after the demo, and never on the doorstep. Trial first. |
| "Internet jaay to?" | "Order DB ma save thay chhe; net aavtaj screen update thay. Settle karvanu kaam atke nahi." |

Never promise: printing (there is none), GST filing (Jamavat prints GST on the
invoice, it does not file returns), Zomato/Swiggy integration (not built).
A promise you cannot keep costs you the next five restaurants in that street.

## 5. After the visit

The second contact is what sells. HQ does this now:

- **HQ → Leads → Add a lead** with a **"Come back on"** date, there and then.
- The **Due** tab is the morning list: every lead whose day has come, oldest
  first.
- **WhatsApp** on each lead opens the chat with the ગુજરાતી pitch already
  written — read it, change what you want, press send yourself. Send the
  45-second cut from `MARKETING_VIDEO.md` with it.
- **Call** dials. Mark **Contacted** so you can see what's been sitting.
- Not interested → **Not interested**, with the reason in Notes. Three "too
  expensive" in one week is a pricing answer, not three dead leads.

A rhythm that works: visit → WhatsApp the video the same evening → call after
three days → visit again in two weeks. Four touches, then leave it.

## 6. The first yes

Set the restaurant up **in front of them**, from HQ: restaurant, trial, invite
link on WhatsApp. Put their real menu in, print the QRs for two tables, and
let them run those two tables for a week. A restaurant that has taken ten real
orders through Jamavat does not cancel.

Then ask for the two things worth more than the subscription: a **Google
review** with the restaurant's name in it, and the **name of one other owner**
they know. That is how the second ten customers arrive without a scooter.
